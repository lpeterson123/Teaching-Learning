How to use this model

Brain regions view: click any region to see its five-layer learning pathway—from neural system to classroom practice.

Neural pathways view: click any circuit to see how two or more regions communicate, and why that connection matters for learning.

Each pathway shows the brain system, the cognitive processes it supports, the research-informed practices that connect, specific teacher moves, and student self-regulation strategies—plus the research base, a developmental note, and a myth to retire.

The goal: Teachers understand just enough neuroscience to know why practices work—and how to adapt them.

A note on evidence: the neuroscience here corroborates and explains practices proven in classrooms and behavioral studies—it doesn't replace that evidence. Use the brain as a way to understand and remember why these moves work.
Key references & further reading
Each node uses the dual-anchor principle: one mechanism citation (how the brain does it) and one classroom citation (whether it works with students).

Mechanism (cognitive science & neuroscience): Cowan, Baddeley (working memory) · Sweller (cognitive load) · Squire, Kandel, Tulving, Ebbinghaus (memory) · Anderson, Graybiel/Knowlton (procedural learning) · Posner & Petersen, Kahneman (attention) · LeDoux, Phelps, Arnsten (emotion & stress) · Gross, Lieberman (regulation) · Schultz, Berridge (dopamine) · Shenhav, Botvinick & Cohen (expected value of control) · Schmahmann, Sokolov (cerebellum — emerging) · Dehaene, Wolf, Ansari (reading & number).

Classroom & instruction: Roediger & Karpicke (testing effect) · Cepeda, Pashler (spacing) · Bjork, Rohrer (interleaving / desirable difficulties) · Ericsson (deliberate practice) · Mayer, Paivio (multimedia / dual coding) · Rosenshine (principles of instruction) · Wiliam & Black (formative assessment) · Bloom (mastery) · Hattie, Nuthall (effectiveness) · Goswami (science of reading) · Willingham (knowledge & thinking) · Dunlosky, Deans for Impact, EEF (syntheses) · Wilson et al. (the 85% rule).

Motivation & self-regulation: Deci & Ryan (self-determination) · Bandura (self-efficacy) · Eccles & Wigfield (expectancy-value) · Flavell (metacognition) · Zimmerman (self-regulated learning) · Dweck (mindset — modest effects: Sisk 2018, Yeager 2019) · Duckworth (grit — contested) · Immordino-Yang, Pekrun (emotions in learning) · CASEL / Durlak (SEL).

Translation & skeptic layer — behind the “myths to retire,” evidence tags, and “corroborates, not proves” framing: Tokuhama-Espinosa & Fischer (Mind, Brain & Education) · Bruer (“a bridge too far”) · Howard-Jones, Dekker (neuromyths) · Kirschner (against learning styles / minimal guidance) · the Learning Scientists, Agarwal, Furst, Hendrick (classroom translation).

The regions shown are simplified maps of distributed systems—attention, reward and memory are networks, not single spots. The neuroscience corroborates and explains practices proven in classrooms; it does not replace that evidence.
